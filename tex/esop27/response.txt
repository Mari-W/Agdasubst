1. CONFLUENCE (B)
Reviewer B is very likely right and we withdraw the confluence claim. Agda's check
accepts our rules, but we now also believe the system is in fact not confluent. 
In particular, the overlap between distributivity and the non-left-linear
eta-law is not joinable: ((zero &ˢ η) ∙ˢ (⟨ wkᴿ ⟩ ⨟ˢ η)) ⨟ˢ η₂ rewrites
to η ⨟ˢ η₂ by the eta-law, but distributivity followed by beta-comp turns
the first component into (zero &ˢ η) [ η₂ ]ˢ.

1.1 Updated claim. We rely on consistency alone, which is preserved
because every installed rewrite rule is instantiated with a proof.

1.2 Implications. Without confluence, subject reduction of the combined
reduction relation may fail, so type checking may behave unpredictably.
No false statement becomes provable, and proofs that type check remain
valid. The method has been robust in practice: we applied it in three
further developments (see below) without problems.

1.4 Commitment. We will rewrite Sec 5.1 and correct every line claiming checked confluence.

2. TERMINATION (C-Q2, L120, L123)
A termination measure for the sigma-calculus is available in the explicit
substitution literature (Abadi et al 1989 for the substitution fragment,
Stark 2020 for first-class renamings). Safe (L120)
means preserving consistency. Termination is not
critical: a looping rewrite system is observable as non-terminating type
checking and does no further harm.

3. HOW THE RULE SET WAS FOUND (A-Q3, C-Q1)
Mostly trial and error, guided by the errors of the type checker.
The coincidence laws, for example,
first surfaced as type errors in later definitions and were then
extracted into rewrite rules. We will describe this in Sec 5.

7. GENERALITY (B, A-Q4, C-Q4, C-Q5)
7.1 Dependent Types. We have experimented with System F omega, System F with subtyping and a GV-style session-type
calculus, all non-dependent. In dependent
type theories syntax and substitution must be defined mutual recursively,
so it may well be the laws cannot be proven before they are needed to be definitional to avoid transfer hell. Hence, we do not expect the
recipe to carry over directly.

7.2 An abstract core (C-Q4) is out of scope, but we believe the recipe
supports an Autosubst-style library with definitional equalities for intrinsically scoped (instead of typed) syntax.

7.3 The duplication (C-Q5) is unavoidable intrinsically: stating
substitution at one level already applies substitution from the level below in its
own return type, even stating this would require very dependent
types, which Agda lacks.

8. WHY IS REWRITING FASTER? (C-Q6)
The transfer-hell version elaborates and checks large transport terms.
With rewriting these terms are never constructed. The transfer-hell
version spends about 50% more time elaborating right-hand sides and four
times more in the occurs check.

9. NOVELTY AND PRIOR ART (B)
We will cite Kaposi's TYPES 2023 abstract. It observes that a fully
strict syntax removes transfer hell in the canonicity setting. We
contribute a worked development, the safety analysis, the recipe, and
measurements. We will sharpen the comparison with [9]: McBride derives
renaming and substitution from a single kit-parameterized traversal, so
the operations and their basic lemmas are defined and proven once, but
the substitution laws remain propositional. We instead turn the laws into
definitional equalities; the techniques are complementary. We will also
discuss Chen et al (CPP 2026): they formalise dependent type theory as a
quotient inductive-inductive-recursive natural model in Cubical Agda,
where rewrite rules are not available, so the substitution laws hold
only up to paths. They report that transport hell reappears in the
deeper metatheory and that strictification helps only partially. Their
conclusion, that intrinsic syntax without strict equations remains a
struggle, supports making the laws definitional where the ambient theory
permits it. The related-work section will be reworked accordingly.
